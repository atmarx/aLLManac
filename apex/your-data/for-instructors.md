## What you are holding

<!-- Open concrete.  When your course runs here, the platform accumulates
     records about identifiable students: what they asked, what they built,

     by the university, generated by your coursework.

     exists and the platform did not create it — running the course did. -->

## Where the obligation comes from

<!-- Short and precise, because precision is the respect:
     - FERPA is the federal statute covering education records

## The four switches you control

<!-- Table: switch / what it does / what to consider.

     1. Agent actions (capabilities:) — whether agents in your course can
        call arbitrary external URLs.  Off by default.  The one that can
        send course content off university hardware.  Domain allowlist not
        built yet, so "on" currently means "anywhere."
     2. Sharing — whether students can share agents with each other.  Off by
        default.  Turning it on makes one student's work visible to others,
        which is a disclosure decision.
     3. What goes into knowledge files — anything uploaded to a course agent
        becomes retrievable by everyone who can use that agent.  Rosters,
        graded work, and student writing are the ones to think about twice.
     4. Roster membership — who is enrolled is who has access.  Removal
        revokes access; it does not erase what was already written.

     Each row links to the student-facing page that explains the mechanism. -->

## Things the platform does for you

<!-- Reassurance where it is earned, and only where it is earned:

## Questions worth asking before the term starts

<!-- Short checklist, framed as questions rather than instructions — a
